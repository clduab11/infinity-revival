# Forever We Reign: narrative brief

**Approved title:** Forever We Reign.
**Approved premise:** A soldier fights through the bosses to regain his throne, then discovers that the throne is cursed.
**Ending purpose:** Preserve the curse reveal as the hook for the next game.
**Recorded:** 2026-09-29, from the direct user request in this chat.

## Source of canon

The following is the user's exact premise, retained as source material:

> As for the game name itself, let's call it "Forever We Reign" where the story revolves around a soldier fighting through the bosses to regain his throne, only to find the throne cursed at the end, setting up for the next game (an idea I had that I wanted to get out before I didn't remember it).

Source: direct user request, this chat, 2026-09-29.
This brief records that approval; it does not substitute new lore for it.

## Confirmed narrative commitments

| Element | Canon |
|---|---|
| Game title | Forever We Reign |
| Protagonist | A soldier |
| Immediate objective | Regain his throne |
| Campaign action | Fight through the bosses |
| Final discovery | The throne is cursed |
| Forward hook | The discovery sets up the next game |

The soldier's name, history, and reason for losing the throne are unassigned.
The bosses' personal names and relationship to the throne are unassigned.
The curse's origin, mechanism, effects, and resolution are unassigned.
The next game's title, scope, and story are unassigned.

## Existing production envelope

Retain the original industrial fantasy art direction and bounded campaign:

- Two regions with branching authored routes.
- Four humanoid bosses using one shared skeleton family.
- One sword-and-shield player combat style.
- A storm-battered foundry and coastal machinery district for region one.
- An elevated observatory and weather-engine complex for region two.
- Ceramic armor, worked brass, dark stone, weathered fabric, and restrained luminous glass.

These regions and materials are the existing working art brief.
They are not evidence of a newly approved explanation for the throne or curse.
Existing boss role labels remain production roles until narrative details are approved.
The throne reveal does not add a fifth boss, third region, or additional skeleton family.

## Campaign spine

| Beat | Required story information | Production placement |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Establish the soldier and his objective to regain the throne | Later campaign writing and presentation |
| Journey | Boss encounters advance that objective through the existing regions | Existing routes and four-boss campaign |
| Approach | Bring the objective toward the campaign's final encounter | Existing final region and guardian work |
| Ending | Reveal that the regained throne is cursed | Task 32 campaign ending |
| Sequel hook | Leave the implications of that discovery available to the next game | Ending presentation, without a sequel production commitment |

This is a sequence of approved facts, not a finished script.
Foreshadowing, dialogue, cinematics, and environmental clues require later authoring.
Any such work must preserve the final discovery and avoid inventing the curse's answer.

## Relationship to the earlier working narrative

The approved soldier-and-throne objective replaces the earlier generic player-role sentence
about an expedition fighter restoring access to the damaged weather-control complex.
The industrial fantasy setting remains the visual production foundation.
The earlier refuge/rebirth fiction remains a working systems brief.
Its relationship to the soldier, throne, and curse is an open writing decision.
Do not treat the existing reconstruction fiction as the confirmed cause of the curse.

Earlier receipts, generated concepts, and exact image prompts retain their original wording.
Their historical titles and visual choices describe the material produced at that time.
Future briefs use Forever We Reign and link back to this narrative record.

## Open decisions, deferred without blocking graybox work

- How the soldier's prior claim to the throne is established.
- Why each existing boss stands along his route.
- How the two existing regions connect to the objective.
- How much foreshadowing preserves the ending's impact.
- How the curse is revealed visually and through writing.
- How the ending and existing rebirth progression coexist.

Resolve these during narrative and content production, before their corresponding assets are finalized.
They do not block Task 06 combat mechanics, Task 09 HUD work, or Task 10 prototype qualification.
Task 24 proves one finished boss and route; Task 32 delivers the final guardian and ending.
