# 1. Research findings and design boundaries

## 1.1 What Infinity Blade I establishes

The strongest verified references are the original creator interview, Epic’s official game page, and Epic’s official manual.

| Verified strength | Lesson for this game |
|---|---|
| Swipe combat designed around touch | Make gesture recognition and response the foundation of the project. |
| Tap-to-move, cinematic navigation | Use authored scene nodes and transitions to control framing and production cost. |
| Defense followed by attack opportunities | Build encounters around readable sequences, successful defense, and earned openings. |
| Equipment mastery and character progression | Reward experimentation while preserving progress with preferred equipment. |
| Persistent progression across repeated attempts | Make failed expeditions productive without removing the need to learn encounters. |
| Concentrated visual presentation | Spend rendering and animation budgets on the combatants and the immediate arena. |

The original creator described **one-finger interaction**. That is different from the approved requirement here: holding and operating a phone with the same hand. Portrait ergonomics therefore require their own prototype. [Original creator interview](https://toucharcade.com/2010/11/15/infinity-blade-interview/), [Epic’s Infinity Blade I page](https://www.epicgames.com/infinityblade/en-US/game-info/infinity-blade-i)

**Historical boundary:** the official manual is from 2012 and includes post-launch additions. It documents equipment mastery, defensive actions, spells, progression, and distinct reset modes, but cannot establish the exact December 2010 launch feature set. Multiplayer, Arena, Survival, and later expansion content are outside this project’s launch commitment. [Official manual](https://cdn3.unrealengine.com/Infinity%20Blade%2FIB1%20Feature%20Images%2FIB_Classic_Manual-df04617915af60774705f5739b3abfcf50b2580b.pdf)

## 1.2 Additional combat lessons

These inform implementation without expanding the approved content scope.

| Reference | Adopted lesson |
|---|---|
| Sekiro | Successful defense should build offensive advantage and reward engagement. |
| Punch-Out | Each opponent should present a learnable pattern with distinctive tells and punish opportunities. |
| Dark Souls boss encounters | Establish consistent rules, communicate danger, and make improvement attributable to learning. |
| For Honor | Direction matters only when the player can clearly read and express it. |

These are design interpretations, supported by creator and publisher discussions of the respective systems. [Sekiro producer interview](https://blog.activision.com/sekiro/2019-03/Inside-Activision-Interview-with-Robert-Conkey-Activision-Producer-for-Sekiro-Shadows-Die-Twice), [Nintendo’s Punch-Out discussion](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/punchout/0/2/), [Miyazaki interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/01/28/an-interview-with-fromsoftwares-hidetaka-miyazki/), [Ubisoft directional combat](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-gb/game/for-honor/news-updates/3i9GE9e7XGWHqKQH2wUtZc/for-honor-the-art-of-battle)

## 1.3 Original creative direction

Use an original industrial fantasy setting built around a damaged weather-control complex:

- **Region one:** a storm-battered foundry and coastal machinery district.
- **Region two:** an elevated observatory and weather-engine complex.
- **Visual vocabulary:** ceramic armor, worked brass, dark stone, weathered fabric, and restrained luminous glass.
- **Player role:** an expedition fighter restoring access to the complex.
- **Rebirth fiction:** the refuge reconstructs the fighter’s expedition equipment and preserves accumulated knowledge.

These are working art and narrative briefs. Public names and branding receive a separate clearance and naming pass before store preparation.

Reference material remains in research documentation. It must not become production assets, copied choreography, traced environments, reproduced UI, or derivative character designs.
