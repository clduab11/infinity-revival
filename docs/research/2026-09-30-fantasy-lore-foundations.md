# Research foundations for original Forever We Reign lore

**Date:** 2026-09-30.
**Purpose:** Give the requested original setting a small, defensible set of mythic themes, then turn those themes into new fiction.
**Status:** Research and creative interpretation, not approved canon or implemented content.

The operator requested an original soldier-and-cursed-throne story with the mythic weight and replay appeal of an older mobile duel RPG. The useful research target is sovereignty, obligation, disaster, and remembrance. Searching for a newly popular fantasy universe would produce derivative names and fashionable worldbuilding without improving this game's two-region campaign.

The companion [lore bible](../design/forever-we-reign-lore-bible.md) develops a new industrial fantasy setting from these broad themes. Its characters, institutions, weather engine, oath mechanism, rewards, and sequel possibilities are creative inventions. None are historical claims or adaptations of the sources' characters.

## Sources and what they actually establish

### 1. Hesiod, Works and Days

**Primary text:** [Hugh G. Evelyn-White's 1914 translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/348/348-h/348-h.htm), especially lines 170-201, 212-285, and 383-404.

The poem contrasts just judgments and collective prosperity with violence and collective suffering. It also treats false oaths as wrongdoing and organizes agricultural work around the seasons. These are claims within an ancient poem, not evidence that wrongdoing causes real weather disasters. A separate institutional translation is available from the [Harvard Center for Hellenic Studies](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/hesiod-works-and-days-sb/).

**Creative inference:** Make the kingdom's infrastructure preserve obligations that its rulers tried to ignore. Weather makes the social consequences visible. A ruler's duty is to people, rather than to possession of a magnificent chair.

**Original transformation:** The weather engine records civic promises. Its curse comes from incompatible accumulated promises and a rule that refuses to release their living guarantor. This mechanism is invented for Forever We Reign; it is not Hesiod's account of divine punishment.

### 2. The Sumerian King List

**Primary text in scholarly translation:** [University of Oxford, Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature, composition 2.1.1](https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/cgi-bin/etcsl.cgi?text=t.2.1.1), opening sections 1-39 and 40-94.

The text presents kingship moving between cities, with a flood dividing portions of its account. Its extraordinarily long early reigns belong to its literary representation. They are not used here as literal historical chronology. The [Ashmolean Museum's collection explanation](https://ashmolean.web.ox.ac.uk/sumerian-king-list) identifies the surviving prism and explains the political presentation of successive kingships.

**Creative inference:** The office can outlast its individual holder, while the official record can flatten a complicated past into an apparently uninterrupted succession.

**Original transformation:** The Anchor Seat preserves prior decrees and incompatible institutional wills. The title's plural refers to an office crowded with old obligations. There is no borrowed dynasty, divine name, city, regnal number, or ancient chronology.

### 3. The Flood Tablet, British Museum K.3375

**Primary artifact catalog:** [British Museum collection record for K.3375](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_K-3375).

The museum identifies a seventh-century BCE clay tablet containing part of Tablet XI of the Epic of Gilgamesh, including a flood narrative. Its curator's account describes the disaster, warning, and preservation of life. This source establishes the object's identity and recorded narrative, not a documented global flood.

**Creative inference:** A civilization can organize its institutions around the remembered catastrophe it most fears. Preserving a memory can become useful preparation or a justification for permanent emergency rule.

**Original transformation:** A coastal kingdom builds storm-control infrastructure after a local catastrophe. Later officials treat any limit on its operation as betrayal. There is no copied ark episode, flood survivor, immortality quest, or sequence of released birds.

The ancient works are used as texts and historical artifacts, not as proof of supernatural systems. Their cultures are not merged into a pretend universal religion. The game's names, visual symbols, and institutions follow the existing ceramic, brass, dark-stone, fabric, and luminous-glass production brief.

## What to take from the named game references

The mechanical research into Chivalry II belongs in the combat comparison document, not in this lore research. This document proposes no combat timings, feint rules, stamina model, or new weapon style.

For narrative, retain only general emotional goals: a compact ascent, meaningful rivals, hard-earned mastery, repetition with growing understanding, and a final revelation that changes the meaning of victory. The soldier regaining his throne and finding it cursed comes directly from the operator. Existing games supply no character identities, dialogue, artifact names, bloodline reveal, hidden family antagonist, signature weapon, or copied cosmology.

## Production consequences

1. Tell the curse through the same weather complex used by gameplay: four bosses, two regions, and visible maintenance records. No additional launch geography is needed.
2. Let rivals defend obligations that conflict. Their equipment should communicate that role before an optional lore entry explains it.
3. Use repeatable crafted patterns for collectible gear. A second earned reveal does not mean a unique historical artifact was recovered twice.
4. Keep the final explanation for the ending. Earlier clues establish failures and omissions without announcing the curse.
5. Build sequel room with unresolved consequences of the same mechanism. Do not commit another game, extra launch bosses, or a live-service schedule through a lore paragraph.

## Research limitations and retrieval note

Sources were inspected on 2026-09-30. The Gutenberg text and Harvard and Ashmolean pages opened successfully. The Oxford corpus translation and British Museum artifact catalog were available through their indexed primary-source results; direct browser opening returned an internal retrieval error. The claims above are limited to the exposed text and collection metadata. The inaccessible historical Coronation Oath Act page was not used as evidence.

This research does not establish global uniqueness of the proposed fictional names, commercial clearance, audience response, or appropriate progression pacing. It supplies broad motifs and a traceable separation between source facts, creative inference, and original invention.
