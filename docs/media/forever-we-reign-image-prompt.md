# Forever We Reign: revised GPT-Image prompt

**Status, 2026-09-30:** prompt only. No replacement image has been generated or
accepted. The intended output is README concept art for the planned visual
direction, not a screenshot or evidence of finished gameplay.

**Basis:** the [approved premise](../production/narrative-brief.md),
[melee direction](../design/melee-combat-direction.md), and
[lore proposal](../design/forever-we-reign-lore-bible.md). New lore names and
curse details remain proposals; the prompt uses descriptive roles instead.
The [existing image and provenance](infinity-revival-prototype.md) remain the
historical record.

The existing image's foreground sword reads as right-handed, but the facing
captain's raised sword reads as left-handed. Hidden shield grips, crossed blade
silhouettes, and bright contact effects make the equipment harder to inspect.
The revised composition uses a poised moment before contact so both actors'
hands, weapons, and weight can be read clearly.

## Copy-paste generation prompt

```text
Create one polished portrait 9:16 concept image for Forever We Reign, an original premium offline fantasy action RPG built around intimate sword-and-shield duels. Show the ambition of a high-end cinematic 3D mobile game: believable material response, carefully authored character silhouettes, atmospheric depth, and a clear opponent. This is aspirational art for a README.

SCENE AND STORY
A displaced soldier returns through a storm-battered coastal kingdom to reclaim his throne. Depict one tense duel on a broad rain-wet dark-stone approach to the kingdom's damaged foundry and weather-engine fortress. Make the place feel old, inhabited, and bound by solemn civic promises: repaired ceramic gate plates, worn service banners, brass storm channels set into stone, and narrow luminous glass records built into the architecture. These details suggest accumulated history and duty without readable writing. An elevated observatory and the weather engine recede into the storm beyond the seawall. The architecture feels monumental and purposeful, with a few visible mechanisms. The royal seat and its curse are discovered at the campaign ending, so keep the throne, supernatural possession, and revealed curse out of this early encounter.

EXACTLY TWO COMBATANTS
In the lower foreground, an original human soldier in practical fitted ivory ceramic armor with worked-brass fittings over salt-worn dark cloth. Give him a repaired narrow service sash, one simple original company mark on a ceramic plate, and an asymmetrical shoulder closure. His bearing conveys an experienced officer returning to a difficult duty. Ahead of him, one original armored captain guards the ascent, wearing darker ceramic and restrained brass with weathered fabric and a broad practical shield. Both have credible human proportions, useful armor articulation, and equipment sized for one-handed combat. Their identities, armor, emblems, and weapons are wholly original.

VIEW AND STAGING
Use a three-quarter rear view of the player from slightly behind his right shoulder, at a steady chest-height camera. Keep enough of both arms visible to verify the hands and their attachments. The player's anatomical right hand and sword occupy the lower-right foreground; his anatomical left forearm and shield occupy the lower-left. The captain faces him several paces ahead with a slight three-quarter turn, centered in the upper-middle frame. For the facing captain, the anatomical right sword hand reads on the viewer's left and the anatomical left shield reads on the viewer's right. Exactly one opponent, fixed duel positions, grounded feet, knees softly bent, balanced weight, and open space between the fighters. Keep both heads, both weapon grips, the player's shield attachment, and the captain's full body legible. Avoid dramatic perspective distortion or extreme camera tilt.

HANDS AND WEAPON GEOMETRY ARE THE PRIMARY CONSTRAINT
Both fighters hold exactly one straight one-handed sword in their anatomical RIGHT hand and exactly one shield on their anatomical LEFT forearm. Anatomical handedness takes priority over decorative composition. Each gloved right hand wraps naturally around a single continuous grip below the crossguard; the fingers, thumb, wrist, grip, guard, and blade form one physically coherent assembly. One hilt connects to one straight rigid blade. Each left hand holds its shield's internal grip, with a believable forearm strap. The shield is a separate rigid object attached to that arm.

Show a calm, alert ready stance before either fighter attacks. Keep the sword elbow comfortably bent near the ribs, wrist neutral, hilt beside the right waist, and blade extending forward and gently upward outside the shield. Give each weapon a clear silhouette against open background. Keep the sword and shield clear of the owner's body and each other. The blades do not meet, cross, pass through anyone, or produce an impact effect. Preserve enough separation to inspect the complete hilt-to-tip line and the relationship between hand and forearm. No mid-swing contortion, reversed grip, bent blade, detached hilt, doubled blade, floating weapon, extra arm, extra hand, or fused fingers.

LIGHT, MATERIALS, AND MOOD
Mythic gravity, restrained wonder, and the silence before a committed exchange. Cold storm light and sea mist, a small amount of warm foundry light, worn pale ceramic, dark stone, tarnished brass, wet steel, and textured cloth. Restrained teal light stays within the architectural glass and storm channels. Let a break in the clouds separate the captain's silhouette from the fortress. Keep atmospheric effects behind the actors so hands and weapons remain crisp. Favor grounded fantasy craftsmanship and sparse meaningful detail over decorative machinery, oversized armor, or glowing equipment. The kingdom's history should feel present in its repairs and service marks.

OUTPUT CONSTRAINTS
One uninterrupted tall image, no panels, phone frame, HUD, touch controls, health bars, arrows, labels, title lettering, readable text, logos, or watermark. No other people, creature limbs, fireball effects, blade trails, impact sparks, gore, or camera shake. No recognizable characters, signature weapons, costumes, or emblems from an existing franchise. Prioritize coherent anatomy, correct handedness, plausible sword angles, readable duel distance, and original fantasy worldbuilding over spectacle.
```
